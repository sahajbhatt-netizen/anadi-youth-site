Puja

Before the day begins pulling your attention in every direction, Puja gives you a place to decide where you want to begin.

For a little while, you step out of the stream of messages, responsibilities, plans, worries, and other people's expectations. You sit before Maharaj and deliberately give Him the first part of your day.

That is what makes Puja more than a morning routine. It is a daily meeting with Maharaj.

Helps cultivate
Focus · Devotion · Surrender

Begin the day with Maharaj.

Most days do not remain quiet for very long. Once work, school, family, notifications, decisions, and responsibilities begin, our attention can become fragmented quickly.

Puja gives the day a different starting point. Before everything else begins asking something from you, you spend time with Maharaj. You look toward Him, remember Him, bow before Him, and speak with Him.

That matters because Puja is not simply preparation for a more productive or peaceful day. It is fundamentally relational. You come before Maharaj because He matters, not only because doing so may help everything else go better.

Puja also becomes the first anchor in a larger spiritual rhythm. The day will move on, but Mansipuja, Aarti, Darshan, and Prayer create opportunities to return to the relationship that Puja establishes in the morning.

Several practices, one meeting.

Morning Puja brings different devotional practices together into one dedicated period with Maharaj.

Darshan places Him before our attention. Mala creates remembrance and focus. Pranam gives physical form to humility and surrender. Prayer gives us a place to speak honestly with Him. Each practice has its own purpose, but Puja allows them to work together rather than remain disconnected activities.

That is part of what makes Puja distinctive. You are not simply completing several requirements in sequence. You are approaching Maharaj in several different ways within the same meeting.

There is an established sequence for performing Morning Puja. If you are learning it for the first time, or want to review the full Nitya Niyam step by step, use the practical guide below.

Bring your actual life with you.

Puja does not need to happen in a separate spiritual world.

If there is a conversation you are worried about, bring it into prayer. If something went well yesterday, thank Maharaj. If you made a mistake, acknowledge it. If you are struggling with a particular reaction, habit, or decision, place that before Him too.

The sixth Pranam creates a particularly personal moment for this. It gives you time to speak sincerely with Maharaj, seek forgiveness, and ask for His guidance and protection.

That connection between the practice and your actual life is important. Puja is not meant to remain behind when you stand up. What you bring before Maharaj in the morning can become something you remember when the same issue, opportunity, or challenge appears later in the day.

Try it

During your next Puja, notice the transition between its different parts.

When you move from Darshan to Mala, or from Mala to Pranam, notice what changes in the way you are relating to Maharaj.

You are not simply moving through a sequence.

You are spending time with Him in several different ways.

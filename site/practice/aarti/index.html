Aarti

Aarti gives us a regular moment to stand before Maharaj, praise Him, and remember who He is.

The words and tune may be so familiar that we can participate almost automatically. But inside those familiar words are Mahima, refuge, gratitude, Darshan, and recognition of Maharaj as Pragat Purushottam.

Aarti becomes deeper when we stop treating those words as something we simply know and allow them to become something we actually mean.

Stand before Maharaj with Mahima.

The way we participate in Aarti changes when the way we see Maharaj changes.

Jay Sadguru Swāmī repeatedly draws our attention toward His identity. Maharaj is compassionate and powerful. He is the giver of happiness. He is Avinashi, Purushottam, and Pragat. The Aarti speaks about taking refuge at His feet, receiving His compassion, recognizing His presence, and appreciating the extraordinary opportunity we have received.

Those ideas are not separate from the practice. They are what give Aarti its direction.

Repeated Aarti also allows Mahima to deepen over time. A verse you once understood only intellectually may eventually connect with something you have personally experienced. The words remain the same, but your recognition of what they mean can grow.

Let the words and Darshan come together.

The lyrics already tell you where to place your attention.

When you sing “Charaṇ saroj tamārā,” you are speaking about refuge at Maharaj's feet. When you sing “Swāminārāyan sukhadātā,” you are recognizing Him as the giver of true happiness. When you reach “Purushottam pragaṭ nuṁ,” the words call you to recognize who is before you. When you sing “Ā avasar,” they turn toward gratitude.

The words were written by Muktanand Swami, but you are the one singing them now.

At the same time, Aarti is an opportunity for Darshan. Rather than allowing the singing and the Murti to become two separate things, let them reinforce each other. Look toward Maharaj while remembering what you are saying about Him.

You are not singing about a distant Maharaj while standing before someone else.

You are standing before the Maharaj the Aarti is praising.

Try it

Before your next Aarti, choose one line from Jay Sadguru Swāmī and understand what it means.

When that line arrives, look toward Maharaj and actually pray what you are singing.

Choose another line the next time.

Little by little, the Aarti you already know can become an Aarti you increasingly mean.

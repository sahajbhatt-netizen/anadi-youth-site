Practice · Guide

How to Practice

A spiritual practice can be performed correctly and still remain on the surface.

You can complete the Mala, sing the Aarti, finish Puja, do Mansipuja, sit for Darshan, or say a prayer while your mind is somewhere else.

The outward action gives the practice its form. What happens within you gives it depth.

01

Know what you are trying to cultivate.

Before beginning a practice, it helps to know what you are trying to develop through it.

Maybe you are using Mala to steady your attention and remember Maharaj. Prayer may help you express something honestly and surrender what you are carrying. Darshan may help you slow down and look at Maharaj with greater Mahima. Dhyan may take Gnan and attention toward deeper spiritual experience.

The same practice can also serve you differently at different times. What matters is having some sense of what you are trying to do within the action rather than only trying to finish it.

A simple question can help:

What am I trying to cultivate through this practice today?
02

Bring your attention and understanding into the practice.

Almost every practice changes when your attention is actually there.

That does not mean the mind has to stay perfectly still. It will wander. You may suddenly realize that you have been thinking about work, replaying a conversation, or planning the rest of your day.

Notice it and come back.

Meaning also gives attention somewhere to go. Aarti becomes easier to enter when you understand what you are singing. A Pranam means more when the physical action is connected with the Bhav behind it. Darshan changes when you remember who you are looking at rather than simply looking at the Murti.

The outer action and the inner understanding support each other.

03

Do not measure every practice by how it feels.

Some days a practice may feel peaceful, devotional, focused, or deeply personal. Other days you may feel tired, distracted, restless, or very little at all.

The practice can still be worthwhile.

Trying to manufacture a particular feeling can sometimes make practice harder. Instead, create better conditions for it. Slow down. Bring your attention back. Remember what you are doing. Give the practice enough space to happen.

Familiar practices can also become mechanical over time. When that happens, you may not need something new. Sometimes it helps to take one familiar part and see it again.

Understand one line of Aarti more deeply. Give one Pranam your full attention. Do one round of Mala carefully. Spend a little longer actually looking during Darshan.

A small part done with greater understanding can reopen a practice that had started feeling automatic.

04

Let the practice follow you into life.

What you cultivate during practice should gradually become easier to access outside of it.

Prayer may change how you respond when something goes wrong. Mala may make it easier to bring scattered attention back. Darshan may help Maharaj return to your awareness during the day. Aarti may deepen Mahima. Mansipuja may make your relationship with Maharaj feel more present beyond the time set aside for the practice itself.

Life also shows you what the practice is actually developing.

If you are practicing surrender, notice what happens the next time something does not go your way. If you are developing attention, notice how easily you can bring yourself back when distracted. If you are trying to deepen your relationship with Maharaj, notice whether He comes to mind more naturally throughout the day.

That gives you something useful to reflect on and bring back into the next practice.

Try it

Choose one practice already in your life.

Before you begin, ask:

What am I trying to cultivate?

While you practice, notice when your attention leaves and bring it back.

Afterward, ask:

What happened within me?

You may notice something that helped, something that became mechanical, or something you want to understand better next time.

Keep working with the same practice for a while. Depth develops as you understand it better, give it more attention, and allow what it cultivates to become part of the way you live.

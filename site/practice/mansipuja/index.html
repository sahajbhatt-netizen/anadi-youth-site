Mansipuja

The day has a way of carrying our attention away. We move from one responsibility to another, respond to people, solve problems, check our phones, think ahead, replay what already happened, and suddenly hours have passed.

Mansipuja gives us a deliberate opportunity to return. Through Manasik Seva, we bring Maharaj back into our attention and spend time with Him in the middle of a day that might otherwise completely absorb us.

Helps cultivate
Focus · Devotion

A practice of returning.

Puja begins the day with Maharaj. Mansipuja creates points of reconnection after the day has already started.

Whatever you were doing before, you pause and turn your attention toward Him again. Then life continues. Later, you return again. Those repeated moments help Maharaj remain part of the day rather than someone we remember only during one protected period in the morning.

The final Mansipuja can become a meaningful way to close the day as well. You return to Maharaj with what happened, what you are grateful for, what you struggled with, and anything you still need to place before Him.

The circumstances around you may not change each time you do Mansipuja. What changes is where you deliberately place your attention.

Use the mind for Seva.

Our minds already spend enormous amounts of time imagining. We replay conversations, anticipate future situations, revisit memories, and picture things that have not happened yet.

Mansipuja gives that same capacity a spiritual direction.

Through Manasik Seva, you mentally spend time with Maharaj. You see His Murti, serve Him, offer something to Him, sit with Him, and interact with Him through the established form of the practice.

The imagination itself is not the destination. It gives the mind a way to participate in devotion.

Let the Seva become personal.

Mansipuja becomes more than visualization when the Seva begins carrying Bhav.

When you serve Maharaj, there can be care. When you offer something, there can be gratitude. When you sit with Him, there can be affection. When you bow, there can be humility. When you speak, there can be honesty.

Detail can help the mind stay engaged, but detail should serve the relationship. The purpose is not to construct the most impressive mental scene possible. It is to spend time with Maharaj.

Your actual life can enter that relationship too. If you are grateful, allow that gratitude into the Seva. If something difficult happened, speak with Him. If you noticed something about yourself, acknowledge it. The point is not to turn Mansipuja into ordinary thinking about your problems, but to keep the rest of your life connected to Maharaj.

Sometimes the most meaningful realization may simply be:

Maharaj is with me in this too.

Try it

During your next Mansipuja, choose one moment of Manasik Seva and stay there a little longer than you normally would.

Notice Maharaj. Notice what you are offering. Notice what that act of Seva is expressing.

You are not trying to make Mansipuja more elaborate.

You are allowing it to become more personal.

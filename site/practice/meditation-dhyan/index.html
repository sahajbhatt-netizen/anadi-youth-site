Meditation & Dhyan

Meditation can help us slow down, become more focused, and notice what is happening within us. Those things are valuable, but Dhyan in our Gnan has a deeper spiritual purpose.

Dhyan is the practice of bringing our inner attention, our Chitta, toward Shriji Maharaj's divine Murti. It helps take something we understand through Gnan and gradually bring it into our experience.

The aim is not only to become better at concentrating. It is to learn to experience Maharaj and ourselves from the spiritual reality our Gnan teaches.

Dhyan gives attention a direction.

Our attention moves constantly. A memory appears. Something we need to do comes to mind. We begin replaying a conversation, worrying about the future, or following one thought into another.

Dhyan gives that attention somewhere to return: Maharaj's Murti.

This is different from trying to make the mind completely blank. Thoughts may still appear. The mind may still wander. When you notice that it has gone somewhere else, you bring the Chitta back toward Maharaj.

That return is part of the practice.

It also develops awareness. You begin noticing where your attention goes without needing to follow every thought or become frustrated that it appeared. A worry can be noticed. A desire can be noticed. A distraction can be noticed. Then you return.

Sometimes a distraction keeps appearing strongly enough that it deserves attention later. Perhaps the same conversation keeps replaying, or your mind repeatedly returns to something you are afraid of losing. Dhyan is not the time to analyze all of it. Bring your attention back to Maharaj. Later, Nij-Darshan can help you understand why that particular thought had so much power over your attention.

Gnan gives Dhyan meaning.

Where we place our attention matters, but so does what we understand about the one we are meditating on.

Our Gnan teaches us who Shriji Maharaj is, who we are, and what we have attained. That understanding gives Dhyan its direction.

We learn to understand Maharaj as the one Sanatan God, eternally Sakar and divine. We begin separating our identity from the changing body, personality, thoughts, emotions, and roles through which we usually experience ourselves. Our Gnan then takes that understanding further toward our spiritual identity as Anadimukta within Maharaj's Murti.

Prapti gives certainty about what Maharaj has given us and the spiritual reality toward which we are growing. Dhyan helps us stop leaving that understanding only at the level of knowledge.

Darshan becomes important here as well. We first learn to look at Maharaj, recognize His Murti, and understand who is before us. Dhyan gradually brings that same Murti into our inner attention.

Gnan tells us what is true. Dhyan gives us a way to begin living from it.

Pratilom Dhyan goes deeper.

Most of us naturally experience spirituality from a simple point of view: I am here, Maharaj is there, and I am looking toward Him.

That is an important beginning.

Pratilom Dhyan takes the understanding of the Self further. Our Gnan teaches the highest spiritual identity as Anadimukta within Maharaj's Murti. In Pratilom Dhyan, we learn to meditate from that identity, understanding the Self within His divine Murti rather than continuing to experience ourselves only from outside of Him.

This is a deeper change than becoming more concentrated. It changes the place from which we understand ourselves.

The deeper Latak of Pratilom Dhyan is learned through the guidance of the Satpurush. Knowing the idea is not the same as knowing how to practice it fully. As our Gnan and foundation become clearer, guidance helps us move into that deeper experience correctly.

Begin with what you understand clearly.

You do not need to force yourself immediately into the deepest form of Dhyan.

Begin with Maharaj's Murti. Take attentive Darshan. Spend enough time actually seeing Him that the Murti becomes familiar to your attention rather than remaining a vague image.

Before you begin Dhyan, recall enough Gnan to know what you are doing. Who is Maharaj? Who am I? What have I attained? You do not need to mentally recite an entire philosophy lesson. Let the understanding give direction to the practice.

Then bring the Chitta toward Maharaj.

When your attention moves, notice it and come back. If it moves again, come back again. Some days your attention may remain more easily. Other days you may spend much of the practice returning.

Keep returning.

As your understanding grows, Darshan becomes clearer, and you receive deeper guidance, the way you practice Dhyan can deepen with you.

Dhyan moves Prapti toward Stithi.

There is a difference between knowing a spiritual reality and becoming established in it.

Prapti gives us the understanding and conviction of what has been attained. But we may still spend most of ordinary life experiencing ourselves through the body, personality, thoughts, reactions, and circumstances around us.

Dhyan works on that gap.

Through deeper Dhyan, especially Pratilom Dhyan, the identity given through Gnan is brought increasingly into experience. The direction is toward Stithi: becoming established as Anadimukta within Maharaj's Murti, Rasbas in His divine bliss.

That is why the destination of Dhyan is deeper than a few peaceful minutes.

Calm can help. Focus can help. Awareness can help. But they prepare the inner life for something greater: moving from knowing the spiritual reality our Gnan teaches toward actually experiencing and becoming established in it.

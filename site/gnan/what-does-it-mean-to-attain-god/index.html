What Does It Mean to Attain God?

If God is the Sanatan, Pragat Cause behind reality, what would it even mean to attain Him?

We normally attain something by getting something we did not have before. We attain a degree after completing school. We attain a position after working toward it. We travel somewhere and say we have reached the destination.

God is different. If He is the final Cause sustaining our existence in every present moment, then He was never completely absent from our life to begin with.

So most of Prapti is not about physically getting something that was far away. It is about recognizing what has already been given to us and understanding what we actually have.

You can have something without knowing what you have.

Imagine a child finds a diamond.

The child may carry it around, play with it, and think of it as a shiny rock. The diamond is genuinely in the child's hand, but the child does not understand what it is or what it is worth.

Then someone who knows diamonds explains it. This is not just a shiny stone. It is a diamond. It is rare. It is worth an enormous amount. What you have been carrying around casually is something people spend years searching for.

The object has not changed. What has changed is the child's recognition of it. Something similar happens with Prapti.

We may hear the name Shriji Maharaj. We may take Darshan, attend Sabha, pray, or participate in Satsang. But the significance of that relationship depends on whether we understand who Maharaj actually is.

That is why Gnan comes first. Once we understand Maharaj as the Sanatan God, the final Cause, Saday Sakar, Divya, and Pragat, the relationship means something entirely different.

We are no longer simply saying, I believe in God.

We recognize:

This Maharaj I have received is that God.

That recognition is at the heart of Prapti.

Recognition requires someone who can recognize Him.

There is an important problem with the diamond analogy.

What if nobody around the child knows what a diamond is? The child cannot recognize its value simply by staring at it longer. Someone who actually knows diamonds has to identify it.

Our Gnan places the Satpurush in that role.

We cannot manufacture an accurate understanding of God entirely from our own present experience. Scripture preserves Maharaj's words and the Gnan of those who experienced Him, but those words still have to be understood. The Satpurush is an Enlightened Anadimukta who knows Maharaj through experience and can therefore teach us what we are actually looking at.

There is a traditional teaching in our Gnan that captures the importance of this: God is not difficult to find and obtain; the one who can lead us to God, the Satpurush, is difficult to find and obtain.

The difficulty is not that God suddenly becomes absent until a Satpurush arrives. Maharaj remains Pragat. He remains the final Cause sustaining us.

What we lack is the recognition. The Satpurush gives us the Gnan through which we can recognize Maharaj correctly. He helps what may begin as an idea become Nishchay.

Without that recognition, we may have the diamond in our hand and continue seeing only a stone.

Pragat and Pratyaksh are connected, but they are not quite the same.

The previous page introduced Maharaj as Pragat.

Pragat means Maharaj is ever-present. As the final Cause, He is not only responsible for some distant beginning of reality. His presence and power sustain reality now.

Pratyakshpanu takes that idea one step closer to us. Pratyaksh means that the God who is always present has made Himself available to be directly recognized and related to.

Our Gnan teaches that Maharaj makes His Gnan known through His words and scriptures, guides us through the Satpurush, and gives us His Darshan through the Murti. When we stand before the Murti, the understanding is not that we are looking at a picture or symbol that merely reminds us of God.

The Murti is Maharaj giving us Darshan in that form.

God is the Cause of the material from which the Murti is made, just as He is the Cause behind everything else. But Pratyakshpanu means more than saying God somehow supports the Murti from behind.

Maharaj has chosen that form as a way to make Himself seen, approached, worshipped, remembered, and loved. That is why idol worship means something very different from worshipping an object. We are not worshipping stone, metal, paint, or an artistic representation of someone who is somewhere else.

We are taking Darshan of Maharaj Himself as He has made Himself Pratyaksh to us through the Murti.

Nishchay makes Prapti firm.

At first, spiritual ideas can remain possibilities.

Maybe God is like this. Maybe this is Maharaj. Maybe the Murti is more than what I can physically see.

Nishchay is when that understanding becomes firm. It is not simply knowing the correct answer to a question. It is a settled understanding that no longer keeps changing every time our mood, circumstances, or doubts change.

I understand who Maharaj is.

I understand that He is Pragat.

And I understand that the Murti before me is not merely representing Him. This is the Maharaj I have been learning about.

This is sometimes called Pratyakshpane Nishchay: the firm recognition that the Maharaj I am having Darshan of is the same divine Maharaj our Gnan has been describing.

That is when Prapti becomes deeply personal. God is no longer only the final Cause of reality. He is my Maharaj.

Prapti changes what we are still searching for.

A great deal of life is spent with the feeling that something is still missing.

One more achievement. One more relationship. More recognition. More security. More money. More certainty about the future. Somewhere underneath the search is often the hope that eventually we will reach a point where we can finally say, Now I have enough.

Prapti gives us another basis for that sense of fullness. If I have attained the very Source behind everything else I could attain, then the most fundamental thing is no longer missing.

Purnakam is the feeling that I am complete or fulfilled. The highest thing I could ultimately seek has already been given to me.

This does not mean we stop having goals, caring about relationships, building a career, or improving our lives. Those things can still matter greatly.

They simply no longer have to make us complete.

Prapti becomes Ashro.

The previous page described Maharaj as the final Cause: the independent Source behind all dependent causes.

Prapti makes that Gnan personal. The One I understand as the final Cause is not merely an explanation of reality.

I have Him. And I am His.

That is the basis of Ashro, or refuge.

Normally, our sense of security rests on things that can change. Health can change. Relationships can change. Work can change. Plans can fail. People we rely on can disappoint us. Even our own mind can feel completely different from one day to the next.

None of those things is Sanatan. But Maharaj is.

When Nishchay becomes firm, we can return to that understanding when fear or uncertainty appears: Maharaj is the final Cause. Everything happening within my life remains within His power. I have attained Him, and I belong to Him.

The circumstance may not immediately disappear. But our experience of it can change dramatically.

Instead of our mind remaining trapped in What if everything goes wrong?, Prapti brings us back to Whatever is happening, I still have Maharaj.

Fear can give way to security. Restlessness can give way to gratitude. The feeling that something fundamental is missing can return to Purnakam.

This is part of the joy of Prapti.

But Prapti and experience are still different.

Something important remains. We can genuinely have Prapti and still not remain in that experience all the time.

One day we may remember clearly: I have Maharaj. I am His. The next day, one conversation, failure, fear, or disappointment may pull our entire attention back into the body and the circumstances around us.

The Prapti did not disappear. Our Stithi changed.

This is the difference the next part of the journey has to address. Prapti gives us the firm recognition of what we have attained. But our identity may still remain rooted in the body, personality, thoughts, and circumstances through which we have spent our whole life experiencing ourselves.

So the next question is not whether we have Maharaj.

It is: How can what I have attained become the reality I actually experience?

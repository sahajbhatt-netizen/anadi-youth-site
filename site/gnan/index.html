Gnan & Philosophy

Some questions sit deep within us.

Who am I? Why am I here? Is there a God? If there is, who is He? How could I actually know? And if I come to understand any of this, can that understanding become something I experience for myself?

Karan Satsang is a journey into those questions through the Gnan of Shriji Maharaj.

It asks us to look carefully at two things: the Self and God. From there comes a third question that sits at the heart of the spiritual journey: what is the relationship between the two?

Follow the journey

You can explore individual questions on their own, but they begin to make more sense when you see how they connect.

Understand the Self

Who am I?

We usually describe ourselves through our body, personality, thoughts, relationships, history, and roles. Our Gnan asks whether any of those things fully explain the one experiencing them.

Why am I here?

Once we begin asking who we are, purpose becomes harder to separate from identity. What is this human life for? What are we here to understand, become, and ultimately attain?

Understand the Relationship

What does it mean to attain God?

The spiritual journey is not only about hoping to reach God someday. Prapti asks us to understand what has already been received and what changes when that attainment becomes clear.

Understanding the Gnan is important, but understanding is not where the journey ends. How can we convert knowledge into a real, lived experience of God?

What is the Anadimukta identity?

“I am Atma” separates the Self from the changing body. Our Gnan goes deeper into the Self's highest spiritual identity and its eternal relationship with God.

Explore the Anadimukta Identity →

The bigger picture

It is easy to study each of these subjects separately. The Self can become one topic, Maharaj another, Prapti another, and Dhyan another.

But they belong to one journey.

We are trying to understand who we truly are, who Maharaj truly is, and the relationship that has always existed between the two. Our Gnan gives that journey direction. Reflection helps us examine where we are actually living from. Practice gives us a way to work with what we understand. The Satpurush helps guide us beyond the limits of our own present experience.

Eventually, the question becomes less about how much philosophy we know and more about what has become real for us.

Guidance

Get Guidance

Sometimes you do not need another article, another framework, or more time thinking by yourself.

You need a conversation with someone who can listen, understand what is happening, and help you see something you may be missing.

That is what this is for.

You do not need to know exactly what kind of guidance you need before reaching out. Maybe you have a specific question about school, career, family, relationships, habits, Satsang, or your spiritual life. Maybe you are trying to make a difficult decision. Maybe the same problem keeps repeating and you cannot understand why. Or maybe you simply feel stuck and want another perspective.

Any of those are enough reasons to start a conversation.

You do not have to figure everything out first.

It can be tempting to wait until you can explain the problem perfectly.

You may think you need to understand exactly what is wrong, organize the whole story, or know what you want to ask before speaking to someone. But sometimes the reason you need guidance is precisely because you cannot yet make sense of what is happening.

You can begin there.

I don't know why this keeps affecting me. I have two options and I cannot tell what I should prioritize. Things at home have been difficult and I do not know how to handle it. I feel disconnected from Satsang. I understand what I should be doing spiritually, but I am struggling to actually do it.

A good conversation can help turn something vague into something you can begin to understand and work with.

When you reach out, share enough context for the person speaking with you to understand the situation. You do not need to tell your entire life story, and you do not need to make yourself more vulnerable than you are ready to be. Start with what feels relevant and honest.

Depending on what you need, the right person may be an experienced devotee, mentor, or Pujya Sant. Different people can help with different kinds of questions, and sometimes the first conversation helps clarify what kind of guidance would be most useful next.

You do not have to work that out alone before beginning.

Start with one conversation.

Getting guidance does not mean committing yourself to a formal mentoring relationship.

Start with one conversation.

Bring the real situation as best as you can. Ask the question you are actually struggling with. Be willing to hear a perspective that may be different from your own.

Then see what becomes clearer.

Sometimes one conversation is enough to help you move forward. Sometimes it reveals that there is something worth continuing to work through. You may decide to speak again, connect with someone who has more relevant experience, or take something from the conversation into your own life and see what happens.

If you receive something useful, try it. Pay attention to what you learn. If you need more guidance afterward, come back with that new experience.

Guidance becomes most useful when the conversation becomes part of what you do next.

If you want to prepare before reaching out, How to Be a Good Mentee explores how to share honestly, receive feedback, try what you learn, and get more from the guidance available to you.

Prayers & Kirtans

Sometimes the right words already exist.

You may want to thank Maharaj but not know how to say it. You may be carrying something difficult and need somewhere to place it. You may want to feel closer to Him, remember His Mahima, surrender something you cannot control, or simply sit quietly with a prayer that expresses what you are feeling better than you can.

Our prayers and kirtans give us those words.

This library is here to help you do more than sing them. Explore what they mean, what they are trying to awaken within you, and how they can become part of your own spiritual life.

New to the pronunciation marks?

Some lyrics use marks such as ā, ī, ṇ, and ḍ to help preserve the pronunciation of the original Gujarati, Hindi, or Sanskrit words.

Learn how to read diacritics →

Find something for where you are.

Different prayers meet us in different places.

Some help us express gratitude. Some strengthen faith when life feels uncertain. Some help us appreciate Maharaj's Mahima. Some express surrender, longing, joy, or the desire to remain close to Him.

You do not need to begin by learning everything. Start with what speaks to something you are experiencing right now.

Gratitude & Mahima

Prayers and kirtans that help you recognize what you have received and appreciate Maharaj more deeply.

Faith & Support

For moments when you need to remember that you are not carrying life by yourself.

Prayer & Surrender

Words for speaking honestly with Maharaj, placing something in His hands, and learning to let go of what you cannot control.

Love & Remembrance

Kirtans that help bring Maharaj back into your attention and deepen the affection behind your spiritual practice.

Spiritual Understanding

Prayers and kirtans whose words teach Gnan, identity, Upasna, or a deeper way of seeing the spiritual journey.

Swaminarayan Aarti

Jay Sadguru Swāmī

Written by Muktanand Swami, the Swaminarayan Aarti is more than something we sing at a particular time each day. Its words express Maharaj's Mahima, surrender at His feet, His compassion, His divine nature, and the significance of having attained Him.

Explore the story behind the Aarti, what each verse means, and how understanding words such as “Charaṇ saroj tamārā” and “Purushottam pragaṭ nuṁ” can change the way you participate in Aarti.

Mere To Ek Tum Hī Ādhārā

You are my only support.

This prayer by Muktanand Swami expresses an extraordinary kind of trust: when circumstances become uncertain and there seems to be nothing else left to rely on, Maharaj remains.

The kirtan begins:

“Mere to ek tum hī ādhārā”

You alone are my support.

Later, Muktanand Swami sings:

“Tum moko miliyo to ānand atishe.”

Its words are especially powerful when you are worried, overwhelmed, afraid, or trying to surrender something that is outside your control.

We have the Gujarati lyrics and English transliteration available, and we will add the full meaning and spiritual context as this entry is developed further.

Gujarati prayers & kirtans

Many of the prayers and kirtans that have shaped our Satsang were written in Gujarati, Hindi, or other Indian languages.

Knowing the words is not always the same as knowing what they mean. A kirtan you have heard hundreds of times can become completely different once you understand what the writer is actually saying, what spiritual state the words are expressing, and why those words might matter in your own life.

We will continue adding prayers and kirtans here with the original lyrics, English transliteration, accessible meanings, spiritual context, and guidance for using them in your own practice.

Do not only learn the meaning.

Understanding a prayer is valuable.

Using it is different.

Take one line into Puja. Remember it during Mansipuja. Return to it when something difficult happens. Sing it when gratitude comes naturally. Use it when gratitude does not come naturally. Let a line become part of a conversation with Maharaj rather than something you only recognize when the music starts.

Over time, familiar words can begin carrying your own experiences too.

That is when a prayer stops being something someone else wrote.

It becomes something you pray.

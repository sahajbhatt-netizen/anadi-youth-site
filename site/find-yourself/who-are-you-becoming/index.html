Who Are You Becoming?

Understanding yourself is not only about discovering who you already are. You are also constantly becoming someone.

Some of that happens without much thought. You grow older, enter different environments, take on responsibilities, meet new people, succeed at some things, struggle with others, and slowly change.

But you also have some say in that process.

You can decide what kind of person you want to become, what qualities you want to develop, what weaknesses you need to work with, and what deserves more of your effort.

You do not need a perfect picture of your future. You need enough direction to stop leaving all of it to accident.

What Is Happening Inside You?

What does progress actually mean to you?

It is easy to describe the future through achievements: a degree, career, income, relationship, home, recognition, or some other milestone.

Those things can matter. But they do not completely describe the person living that life.

Two people can reach the same goal and become very different people along the way. One may become more dependable, disciplined, generous, and capable. Another may reach it while becoming exhausted, dishonest, or disconnected from everyone around them.

Thinking about who you are becoming means looking at both.

Pause with this
Imagine yourself three years from now. What would you genuinely like to be different about you?What qualities would you like the people closest to you to experience more consistently?What would you like to be more capable of handling?Which parts of your idea of progress are about what you want to have, and which are about who you want to become?
Try it

Write down three sentences beginning with:

I want to become someone who…Avoid achievements for this exercise.Describe qualities, abilities, or ways of living.

Keep the three somewhere visible for a week. At the end of the week, ask yourself whether they still feel important or whether you would change them.

01

Know what you have to work with.

Growth becomes easier when you have an accurate picture of yourself.

Some abilities come naturally to you. Maybe you connect easily with people, stay calm under pressure, organize things well, learn quickly, notice details, or bring energy into a group. Those strengths become much more useful once you recognize and develop them deliberately.

Weaknesses deserve the same honesty. You may avoid difficult conversations, lose motivation quickly, overthink decisions, become defensive, struggle with consistency, or take on more than you can manage.

Knowing that does not require turning yourself into a list of flaws. It gives you something real to work with.

Pause with this
What do people regularly rely on you for?What kinds of situations tend to bring out your best qualities?Which strength do you think you have developed well, and which one are you underusing?What difficulty or weakness keeps creating similar problems for you?When that weakness shows up, what usually happens next?
Try it

Choose one strength and one weakness to observe for the next week.

Do not try to fix either one yet. Just notice when they appear.

At the end of the week, write down one situation where your strength helped and one where your weakness made something harder. You may learn more from seeing them in context than from simply labeling yourself.

02

Values become clearer when they cost something.

Most of us can name qualities we admire: honesty, kindness, discipline, loyalty, courage, humility, responsibility.

It is harder to know how deeply we value them until two things we care about compete.

Honesty feels different when telling the truth may disappoint someone. Responsibility becomes more meaningful when admitting a mistake would be embarrassing. Kindness becomes harder when you are already irritated.

Those moments give us a clearer picture of the values we are actually living by.

Pause with this
Which qualities do you most admire in other people?Which of those would you genuinely like to become more known for yourself?Think of a recent difficult choice. What two things were competing?What did your decision reveal about what mattered most to you in that moment?Is there a value you talk about more easily than you currently live it?
Try it

Choose one value you want to pay closer attention to this week.

Do not create a large goal around it. Just notice one moment each day when living by that value is slightly inconvenient.

At the end of the week, look back at what you actually chose.

03

Learn how you make progress.

Pick something you already want to do: exercise more regularly, finish a book, learn a skill, prepare for an exam, save some money, improve a relationship, or finally complete something you have been putting off.

Working toward a real goal shows you what tends to help you move and where you usually get stuck. You may start strongly and lose interest, work better with a deadline, avoid the hardest part, need someone to keep you accountable, or discover that something is easier once you simply begin.

Those patterns are useful to know.

Pause with this
What is one thing you have wanted to make progress on?Have you tried before? What happened?What usually helps you keep going?Where do you tend to get stuck or stop?
Try it

Choose one small goal you can work toward over the next one or two weeks.

When you finish, or when the time is up, look back:

What helped me move forward?What kept getting in the way?What would I do differently next time?

Over time, you can start learning how you make progress.

Direction can change.

You are not choosing the final version of yourself.

As you gain experience, some goals will matter less. You may discover strengths you did not know you had. A responsibility may change your priorities. Something you once considered success may no longer feel important.

That does not mean the earlier direction was useless. Part of growing is becoming capable of examining your direction and adjusting it.

Nij-Darshan helps with that. Instead of only asking whether you are making progress, you can keep looking at what the process is revealing about you.

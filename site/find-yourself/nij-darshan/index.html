Find Yourself · Practice

Nij-Darshan

Take a pause with yourself.

Life moves quickly. Something happens, we react, and then we move on. Over time, the same frustrations, fears, relationship problems, habits, or decisions can keep returning without us ever really understanding why.

Nij-Darshan is the practice of looking within. It is an honest conversation with yourself about what you are experiencing, why you may be experiencing it that way, and what it can teach you about yourself.

Sometimes it helps you understand something that went wrong. Other times, it helps you understand a difficult decision, a repeated pattern, something that went well, or simply what you are feeling.

Start with something real.

You do not need to invent a deep question. Start with whatever already has your attention. Maybe a conversation is still bothering you. You keep avoiding a decision. You feel stressed and are not completely sure why. Something went unusually well. The same issue has appeared again in a relationship.

Begin by separating what happened from what happened inside you. What was actually said or done? What did you think it meant? What did you feel? What were you expecting?

Then ask the next question that naturally follows.

If someone ignored you and you became angry, you might realize that it felt disrespectful. Then you can ask why their respect mattered so much to you. Maybe you discover that this particular person's opinion carries more weight than you realized.

Sometimes the first answer is enough. But when something keeps repeating, stays with you longer than expected, or feels unusually intense, it may be worth looking a little deeper.

A useful sequence is:

NoticeWhat happened?
Look inwardWhat happened inside me?
Ask whyWhy?
Look for the patternHave I seen this before?
RespondWhat do I want to do with what I learned?

See what that looks like.

Imagine someone gives you critical feedback and you spend the rest of the evening replaying the conversation.

You could stop at, “They criticized me and I got upset.”

Or you could keep going. Which part bothered me most? Did I disagree with what they said, or did I dislike being criticized at all? Would the same feedback have affected me this much if it came from someone else? Do I often become defensive when I feel incapable? Is there anything useful in what they said once I separate the feedback from how it made me feel?

You may still conclude that the person communicated badly. But now you also understand something about yourself.

That is where Nij-Darshan becomes useful.

Find a way that helps you think clearly.

Some people think more clearly when they write. A journal can slow the conversation down and make patterns easier to see. Others find it easier to speak, perhaps through a private voice note. Sometimes sitting quietly with one question for a few minutes is enough.

What matters is staying with the thought long enough to get beyond the first automatic answer.

Nij-Darshan also does not have to become a review of everything wrong with you. Something going well can teach you just as much. You can ask why a difficult conversation felt easier this time, what helped you stay calm, why you enjoyed something more than expected, or what strength showed up when you needed it.

The aim is to see yourself more clearly.

Look, improve, and let go.

Guruji often gives a simple approach:

“Look at it, improve it, and let it go afterwards.”
— Guruji

Nij-Darshan asks you to notice thoughts, emotions, reactions, mistakes, and patterns honestly. But noticing anger does not mean I am anger. Seeing insecurity does not mean this is who I am. A mistake can tell you something without becoming your identity.

Look at what happened. Understand it. Improve what needs to improve.

Then let it go.

Otherwise, reflection can become another way of holding onto the very things you are trying to understand. You keep replaying the mistake, repeating the label, or carrying yesterday's reaction into today.

The purpose is to learn from what you see, not become attached to it.

Bring what you learn back into life.

Reflection becomes useful when it changes the way you return to life.

You may realize that an expectation is unrealistic, that you need to have a conversation, that the same insecurity keeps appearing, that something needs to change, or simply that you want to keep observing before deciding anything.

Then you live again and see what happens. Maybe your new response works. Maybe the same problem appears again. Maybe the next situation shows you something you had missed. That gives you something new to understand.

Nij-Darshan is not separate from ordinary life. Ordinary life gives you the material for it.

Let it deepen with you.

At first, Nij-Darshan may help you understand your emotions, habits, relationships, decisions, strengths, and weaknesses. As your Gnan deepens, the questions can deepen too. You may begin looking at the difference between the changing body, personality, roles, thoughts, and reactions you experience every day and the deeper spiritual identity our Gnan teaches.

There will also be places where your own perspective is not enough. Another person may see something you cannot, or a spiritual question may require understanding beyond your current experience.

That is where guidance becomes important.

Take one situation that is already on your mind and give it ten quiet minutes.

“What is actually happening here?”

Follow the conversation honestly. See what there is to understand, what may need to improve, and what you can leave behind afterward.
